Sky Falcon

A low-cost, four-layer system that tells drones from birds

Student project · In development · SAIF 2026
Why this project

The problem

Drones have become cheap and easy to build, which makes them attractive tools for misuse. The trouble is that ordinary detectors see them much as they see birds, and the result is either a threat that passes unnoticed or a stream of false alarms that erodes trust in the system.

Even on radar, birds and small drones reflect signals of similar strength, so a single sensor is not enough to tell them apart with confidence. That is exactly where this project starts.

Why one sensor is not enough

Camera

Sees shape and motion, but is affected by light, fog and dust, and at distance the object is only a few pixels.

Radar

Detects motion even in darkness, but small drones reflect a weak signal much like a bird does.

RF detection

Long range and low cost, but completely blind to a drone that transmits nothing.

Acoustics

Works without line of sight, but has short range and suffers from wind and ambient noise.

Every sensor has a blind spot, and one sensor's blind spot is not another's. That is the basis of the four-layer design.

The science

Doppler: telling a rotor from a wing

When a radar wave bounces off a moving object, its frequency shifts by an amount proportional to the object's speed. This is the Doppler effect, the same reason an ambulance siren changes pitch as it passes you.

But the key idea here is not the speed of the object itself; it is the motion of its parts. A drone's body moves slowly while its rotors spin very fast, and this produces extra signals around the main return, known as the micro-Doppler signature.

fd = 2 · v · f0 / c

fd frequency shift · v speed of the moving part · f0 radar frequency, 10.525 GHz · c speed of light

Applying this to the HB100 radar: every metre per second of speed gives roughly a 70 Hz shift. A rotor tip moving at 30 m/s gives about 2100 Hz, while a bird wing beating at 5 m/s gives only about 350 Hz.

And the difference is not only in the number but in the shape of the signal: a rotor spins continuously and regularly, giving a steady repeating pattern, whereas a wing beats, pauses and glides, giving a broken, irregular one. Try the tool below.

The physical limit

The radar equation: why range is short

The biggest constraint in this project is radar range. The reason is not a weak design but a physical equation that cannot be argued with: radar range scales with the fourth root of power and antenna gain.

R ∝ ( Pt · G² · σ / Pmin )1/4

R range · Pt transmitted power · G antenna gain · σ target radar cross-section · Pmin minimum detectable signal

A fourth root means doubling the range requires sixteen times the effective power, not twice. This is also why adding more radar units side by side does not help: the units are not phase-coherent, so their signals do not combine, and they may interfere with one another instead.

The only practical route to more range is higher antenna gain and lower receiver noise. Because antenna gain enters the equation twice, on transmit and on receive, it affects range more strongly than an equal improvement in sensitivity. Try it yourself:

Range calculator

Move the sliders to see how a better antenna and amplifier affect the expected range. The baseline is 7 metres for a small drone.

Notice that a 10 dB improvement in the antenna roughly triples the range, while the same improvement in sensitivity not even doubles it. Even so, with the best realistic improvement the range stays in metres, not kilometres, which the results section states plainly.

The design

The four layers

Arranged from far to near. Each layer has one defined task and wakes the next when it spots something. Separating the tasks this way reduces errors compared with relying on a single sensor.

1

RF signal detection

ESP32

This layer listens to surrounding Wi-Fi traffic without transmitting anything. Most commercial drones talk to their controller or stream video over Wi-Fi, and signal strength gives a rough indication of how close the drone is.

Limit: it cannot detect a pre-programmed drone that transmits nothing, nor one using different frequencies or encryption.
50–200 m
2

Camera and Doppler radar

Camera + HB100

The camera tracks the moving object with a motion-detection algorithm while the radar reads the micro-Doppler signature described above. The camera answers "where is it?", the radar answers "what is it?".

Limit: radar range is very short because a small drone reflects a weak signal, and this is the part of the project most in need of improvement.
≈50 m camera, ≈7 m radar
3

Microphone

Microphone

A drone has a distinctive acoustic signature from its rotors, at frequencies different from birds or wind. This layer helps most when vision is poor, at night, in dust, or behind an obstacle.

Limit: it is affected by ambient noise and wind, and its range drops sharply in noisy places.
35–45 m
4

Ultrasonic sensor

Arduino + HC-SR04

Sends an ultrasonic pulse and measures the echo time to compute distance precisely during final approach, confirming the object's position before any action is taken.

Limit: only a few metres, because high-frequency sound waves attenuate very quickly in air.
up to 4 m
A Raspberry Pi 5 combines the layers and issues the decision: drone, bird, or uncertain.
How the system decides

Decision logic

The system never relies on one layer. The core rule is that an object is classified only if at least two layers agree. That rule is precisely what cuts false alarms: one sensor being wrong is not enough to raise an alert.

And if the layers disagree, the system does not guess. It returns "uncertain", which is a correct and useful result, because it calls for human verification instead of offering false confidence.

Where this project stands

Compared with commercial systems

The core idea, fusing several sensors to tell drones from birds, is already used in commercial systems worldwide. This table sets out the difference plainly.

SystemSensorsRangeCost
Robin Radar IRISMicro-Doppler radar≈5 km detection, 2 km classificationUnder one million dollars
DedroneRF, radar, video, acoustic5 km RF, 3 km radar, 1 km cameraTens to hundreds of thousands
DroneShieldRF, radar, acoustic, cameraKilometresTens to hundreds of thousands
Sky FalconRF, radar, camera, acoustic, ultrasonicHundreds of metres for the first layer, metres for the radarUnder 1100 SAR

The honest conclusion: the idea is not new, and the range gap is vast. What distinguishes this project is achieving the same logic at a thousandth of the cost, with a modular design that allows any layer to be upgraded later without rebuilding the system.

Measurement plan

  • Measure radar range before and after fitting the reflector antenna, with ten trials at each distance.
  • Compute the false-alarm rate for the camera alone, then for camera plus radar, over a continuous run.
  • Measure decision time from first detection to issued classification.
  • Repeat the tests under different lighting conditions.
Decisions and reasoning

Development log

This log records the project's engineering decisions, including alternatives that were considered and rejected, because why an option was rejected matters as much as why another was chosen.

First decision

Why fusion instead of a single sensor

Examining each sensor's limits showed that each has a different blind spot, which led to the multi-layer design.

Rejected alternative

Adding more radar units

Placing several HB100 units together to extend range was considered and rejected: the units are not phase-coherent so their signals do not combine, and they may interfere with each other. Coverage increases; range does not.

Rejected alternative

Ultrasonic as the long-range layer

Rejected because ultrasonic waves attenuate very quickly in air, giving a range of only a few metres, unsuitable for early warning. It was kept as a close-range confirmation layer.

Rejected alternative

A conventional long-range radar as the first layer

Theoretically the ideal choice, but direction finding requires a rotating antenna or a coherent array, well beyond the project's budget. RF detection was chosen instead, giving comparable range at a fraction of the cost.

Current decision

Two-layer agreement rule

The rule that an object is classified only when at least two layers agree was adopted to cut false alarms, with an "uncertain" output when they disagree.

Beyond the first prototype

Next phase

After the system is built and tested, the next step is a verification drone carrying the camera and radar. Instead of waiting for a target to come within range of the short-range radar, the drone flies out to check it up close. This turns the range limit from a fixed constraint into a question of movement.

The idea came from my brother, and it raises one core challenge that must be solved: the carrier drone's own rotors produce a strong Doppler signature that interferes with the radar reading. One proposed solution is for the drone to hover and stop manoeuvring at the moment of measurement.
